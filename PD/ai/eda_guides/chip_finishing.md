# Chip Finishing: Metal Fill, Density Analysis, and DRC Clean-Up

## Overview

Chip finishing is the final phase of physical implementation before signoff. It encompasses metal fill insertion, via fill, density analysis and correction, final DRC clean-up, and preparation for tapeout. While often considered a "bookkeeping" step, chip finishing directly impacts yield, manufacturability, and even timing (through parasitic capacitance changes from fill).

Chip finishing errors have caused tapeout delays and even respins. A disciplined, thorough approach to chip finishing is essential.

## Metal Fill

### Why Metal Fill is Required

Chemical Mechanical Polishing (CMP) is used during manufacturing to planarize each metal layer. CMP removes material at a rate that depends on local pattern density. Regions with low metal density experience faster removal (dishing) while regions with high density experience slower removal. Non-uniform density causes:

- **Thickness variation**: Metal thickness varies across the die, affecting resistance
- **Topography**: Uneven surface causes issues for subsequent lithography steps
- **Via connectivity**: Dished metal may not make reliable contact with vias

Foundries mandate minimum (and maximum) metal density on every layer, typically checked in sliding windows across the die.

### Density Requirements

Typical density requirements (vary by foundry and node):
- Minimum metal density: 20-30% per window
- Maximum metal density: 70-85% per window
- Window size: 25x25 um to 100x100 um (multiple window sizes may apply)
- Step size: Half the window size (overlapping windows)
- Density gradient: Maximum difference between adjacent windows (e.g., 20-30%)

### Types of Metal Fill

#### Floating Fill

- Electrically unconnected metal shapes
- Simplest to insert; no routing needed
- Adds parasitic capacitance to nearby signal and clock nets
- May affect timing if inserted too close to critical nets

#### Grounded Fill

- Metal fill shapes connected to VSS (ground) through via stacks
- Provides noise shielding (fill acts as a ground reference)
- Requires via connections to VSS, which consumes routing resources
- Better for signal integrity but more complex to implement

#### Timing-Aware Fill

- Fill shapes are kept away from timing-critical nets
- A keep-out distance (e.g., 0.5-2 um) is maintained around specified nets
- Minimizes capacitive impact on critical paths
- May leave density holes near dense routing of critical nets
- Best practice for production designs

### Fill Insertion Flow

1. **Identify fill regions**: Determine which areas need fill (below minimum density)
2. **Define keep-out**: Specify minimum distance from fill shapes to signal wires
3. **Define timing-aware nets**: Specify critical nets that need extra keep-out distance
4. **Generate fill shapes**: Insert fill shapes meeting minimum size, spacing, and density rules
5. **Verify density**: Re-check density after fill insertion
6. **Extract and verify timing**: Re-extract parasitics with fill and verify timing impact
7. **Iterate if needed**: Adjust fill parameters if density is not met or timing is impacted

### Fill Shape Rules

Fill shapes must satisfy their own design rules:
- Minimum fill shape width and length
- Minimum spacing between fill shapes
- Minimum spacing from fill to signal wire
- Maximum fill shape size (prevents large floating conductors)
- Fill shape must be within the same metal layer design rules

## Via Fill

### Purpose

Via fill inserts additional via connections in areas where they improve density uniformity and reliability:

- Via density rules (similar to metal density rules) ensure uniform via CMP
- Via fill connects floating metal fill shapes on adjacent layers (creating grounded fill stacks)
- Additional vias between power rails on different layers reinforce the power grid

### Via Fill Considerations

- Via fill must not create DRC violations (spacing, enclosure)
- Via fill should not connect different signal nets (short circuits)
- Via fill between power rails on adjacent layers is always beneficial
- Via fill in signal regions must be carefully managed to avoid capacitive coupling

## Dummy Metal (Non-Functional Features)

Beyond fill for density, some processes require specific dummy patterns:

### Dummy Poly

- Dummy poly gates at the edges of active regions maintain uniform poly density and pitch
- Required by lithography for pattern fidelity at row ends and near macros
- Usually handled by endcap cells and standard cell library design

### Dummy Active (OD)

- Dummy oxide diffusion regions maintain OD density
- Typically generated by the foundry's fill utility or included in library cells
- Required for uniform CMP on the active layer

### Metal Slotting

For very wide metal shapes (e.g., power stripes wider than a threshold), slots must be inserted:
- Slots are rectangular holes cut into wide metal
- Slots prevent stress accumulation and improve CMP uniformity
- Slot size, spacing, and pattern are defined by design rules
- EDA tools insert slots automatically for shapes exceeding the width threshold

## Density Analysis

### Pre-Fill Analysis

Before inserting fill, run density analysis to understand the baseline:
- Identify layers with density below minimum
- Identify layers with density above maximum (may need to remove routing)
- Identify density gradient violations (sharp density changes between windows)
- Generate density maps for visual inspection

### Post-Fill Verification

After fill insertion, verify:
- All windows meet minimum density on all layers
- No windows exceed maximum density
- Density gradient between adjacent windows is within limits
- Fill shapes do not violate DRC
- Fill shapes do not create antenna violations

### Density Debug

Common density problems and fixes:
- **Low density in routing channels**: Insert additional fill with smaller keep-out
- **High density in congested regions**: May need to redistribute routing or accept waiver
- **Gradient violations**: Add transitional fill in the gradient region
- **Macro blockage regions**: Fill cannot be inserted inside macros; surrounding fill must compensate

## DRC Clean-Up

### Final DRC Flow

After all optimization, fill insertion, and design changes:

1. **In-tool DRC**: Run the PnR tool's built-in DRC checker for quick feedback
2. **Signoff DRC**: Run the foundry signoff DRC deck (Calibre, Pegasus, IC Validator) for definitive results
3. **Categorize violations**: Sort violations by type, layer, and severity
4. **Fix violations**: Apply automatic and manual fixes
5. **Re-verify**: Run signoff DRC again to confirm fixes

### Common Post-Route DRC Violations

- **Metal spacing**: Usually fixed by nudging wires apart or rerouting
- **Via enclosure**: Fixed by adjusting metal landing pads
- **Minimum area**: Small metal jogs may not meet minimum area; extend or remove
- **Density violations**: Fixed by fill adjustment
- **Antenna violations**: Fixed by diode insertion or rerouting
- **Off-grid violations**: Cells or wires not on the manufacturing grid; snap to grid
- **Endcap/boundary violations**: Missing endcap cells at row ends

### DRC Waiver Management

Some DRC violations may be acceptable (known and approved by the foundry):
- IP blocks may have pre-approved waivers
- Certain corner cases may have foundry acknowledgment
- Document all waivers with foundry communication reference
- Never waive violations without foundry approval

## Timing Impact of Fill

### Capacitance from Fill

Metal fill adds parasitic capacitance to nearby signal wires:
- Coupling capacitance between fill shapes and signal wires
- Grounded fill adds coupling to ground (increases total capacitance but reduces miller coupling)
- Typical timing impact: 1-5% delay increase on affected nets

### Managing Fill Impact

1. **Extract with fill**: Always extract parasitics (SPEF) with fill shapes included
2. **Compare timing**: Run timing analysis before and after fill to quantify impact
3. **Adjust keep-out**: If timing degrades unacceptably, increase keep-out distance around critical nets
4. **Iterate**: Re-fill with adjusted parameters and re-verify timing

### Fill-Aware Extraction

- Signoff extraction (StarRC, QRC) must include fill shapes in the extraction input
- Fill shapes can be extracted as floating conductors or grounded conductors depending on fill type
- The extraction tool determines coupling between fill and signal wires based on geometry

## Tapeout Preparation

### Final Checklist

Before streaming out the final GDS for tapeout:

- [ ] All DRC violations resolved or waived with foundry approval
- [ ] LVS clean (layout matches schematic)
- [ ] ERC clean (electrical rule check)
- [ ] Antenna rules clean
- [ ] Density rules clean on all layers
- [ ] Fill insertion complete and verified
- [ ] Timing signoff at all corners passes
- [ ] Power (IR drop, EM) signoff passes
- [ ] All IP/macro GDS merged correctly
- [ ] Seal ring and scribe lane present
- [ ] Alignment marks and process control structures present
- [ ] GDS/OASIS file generated with correct layer mapping

### GDS Stream-Out

- Verify GDS layer map matches foundry specification
- Check that all required layers are present
- Verify cell hierarchy is correct
- Run foundry-provided GDS validation scripts
- Archive the tapeout database with all associated files

## Practical Guidance

1. **Fill early in exploration**: Run preliminary fill during the optimization phase to understand density feasibility
2. **Timing-aware fill is mandatory**: Never insert floating fill without timing-aware keep-out. The timing risk is not worth the simplicity
3. **Automate the flow**: Script the fill-extract-verify cycle for reproducibility
4. **Budget time for DRC clean-up**: Allow 1-2 weeks for final DRC clean-up on a large design. Do not underestimate this phase
5. **Track DRC trend**: Monitor DRC violation count across implementation iterations. A growing count indicates a methodology problem
6. **Signoff tool correlation**: Verify that the PnR tool's internal DRC matches the signoff tool's results. Address any systematic differences early
7. **Backup before fill**: Save a database checkpoint before fill insertion. Fill can be regenerated if parameters need adjustment
8. **Multi-layer fill coordination**: Fill on adjacent layers should be coordinated to avoid creating large coupling capacitances between fill shapes
